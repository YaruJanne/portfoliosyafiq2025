STEM Robotics with VEXcode VR

A block-coding robotics module I designed and taught as a STEM Education Intern at Micro Concept Tech. Students go from “what is a robot?” to coding a virtual robot that escapes a maze, all in the browser before they touch real hardware.

The module in 50 seconds

ROLECurriculum designer & instructor
AUDIENCEPrimary & secondary students
TOOLSVEXcode VR (block coding), VR playgrounds
FORMicro Concept Tech

How the module is built

The 45-slide deck runs in four parts: how robots work and the types we use, the block-coding basics (events, drivetrain, sensing, controls and operators), getting started in VEXcode VR’s virtual playgrounds, and three coding challenges.

Two guide robots, R1 and R2, appear on every slide to ask questions and talk students through the next step, so the deck can lead the class as well as support it.

Three challenges, rising in difficulty

Castle Crasher: knock down every castle, learning sequencing and debugging through a first trial, adjustments and a timed challenge.

Autonomous Sweeper: on a dynamic castle layout, students build a scanning algorithm, an attack routine and a safety switch that keeps the robot inside the arena.

The Smart Mouse: a wall maze where students start with a “blind” robot, add touch sensing, loops and optimisation, and finish by escaping with the left-hand rule.

Teaching approach

Every lesson is built around trying, failing and fixing: run the code, see what goes wrong, adjust, try again. Working in simulation lets students debug safely and quickly before moving to physical robot kits.

What I produced

The full slide deck, a VEXcode solution project for each challenge, and a library of short screen recordings that demonstrate every block and every challenge step for use in class.
